# Photos and illustrations

## Photos

- **Fit the purpose:** a photo for a travel page shows travel, not groceries.
- **Authentic and human:** use real people doing real things, with restrained emotion. Avoid cliché stock shots.
- **Quality:** high resolution, then compressed. Ship several sizes (S, M, L) and prefer JPG over PNG for photos.
- **One focal point.** Point the subject's gaze or direction toward the CTA or the key content.
- **Tell a story and evoke emotion:** a family cooking together beats a plain shot of cutlery.
- **Keep the set consistent:** a similar tone, or a shared subtle overlay. Product photos are the exception: they must stay accurate.
- **Text over a photo:** always add an overlay for contrast. It can be solid with transparency, or a gradient that is strongest where the text sits. Don't let the overlay hide the photo.
- **Crops:** test every image at 1:1, 4:3, 16:9 and 4:5.
- **Licensing:** check licenses. Unsplash and Pexels are free for commercial use.

## Illustrations

- **Good for:** landing pages that explain abstract ideas, onboarding (the general idea only, not every feature), achievements and badges, **empty states**, and **404 pages** (illustration, short copy and an action).
- **Audience:**
  - Illustrations suit young or playful audiences.
  - Use photos for serious, older or luxury audiences.
  - **Never illustrate the actual products being sold.**
- **Consistency:** one illustration style across the product (use a pack).
- **Size:** never shrink an illustration until its details are unreadable. Use a simpler illustration or an icon instead.
