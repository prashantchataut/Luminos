# Layout, hierarchy, white space

## Hierarchy and layout

### Hierarchy

Every screen needs a clear first, second and third read. These are the levers:

- **Size:** bigger reads as more important.
- **Color:** use bold colors sparingly and soft colors for backgrounds.
- **Weight:** a smaller bold heading takes less space than a large regular one.
- **Position:** top-left comes first for left-to-right readers. Mirror it for right-to-left languages.
- **Proximity:** close elements read as a group.
- **Alignment:** aligned elements read as related. Symmetry looks calm.
- **Common region:** a box or card groups its contents.
- **Figure and ground:** the background is soft and light, and the foreground is meaningful and interactive. Never put a strong saturated color or a raw photo (with no overlay) behind content.

### Web grid

- **Columns:** 12 by default, because 12 divides by 6, 4, 3 and 2. Use 8 for simpler layouts. **Never 5, 7 or 11.**
- **Gutters:** 12–20 px. **Desktop margins:** about 160–180 px at a width of 1440.
- **Column width** = (width − gutters × (columns − 1) − 2 × margin) ÷ columns, rounded. The book's example: (1440 − 11×20 − 2×160) ÷ 12 = 75.
- **Fluid grid** (columns stretch) for general content.
- **Fixed grid / max-width** for forms and long-form reading. A login form or an article must not stretch across a wide monitor.

### Mobile grid

- **Side margins:** 20 or 24 px. Nothing may sit inside them.
- **Spacing:** use the 4 pt scale.
- **Columns:** usually none. When you need columns, use 2 or 4 only.

### Alignment and edges

- **Align text by baseline,** not by vertical center. This applies to the first lines of neighboring text blocks and to a heading next to a button.
- **Keep text and elements away from screen edges and corners.** The only exceptions are the top nav and the footer.

## White space and simplicity

### White space

- **White space creates focus.** The same elements with more space between them look more professional.
- **Start with too much space, then tighten** step by step. Starting tight and adding space later ends at "good enough".
- **Don't fill empty space** without a reason. Pushing back on requests to "fill that empty area" is valid.
- **Separate content with space** rather than divider lines, where possible.

### Simplicity

- **A UI is minimal when nothing can be removed** without losing function. Every element needs a reason to be there.
- **Start with the simplest solution,** then add quality-of-life features one at a time.
- **Minimal is not empty or boring.** Keep the personality through type, color, radius, soft shadows and blur.
